# Introduction

Under the HORIZON-CL6-2021-GOVERNANCE-01-17 call, four projects were
selected to illustrate the potential of environmental and climate data
through dedicated European data spaces in line with the objectives of
the European Green Deal and the European strategy for data. To this
purpose, each project set up a suite of use cases, deploying digital and
data technologies as key enablers to sketch their vision of the Green
Deal Data Space (GDDS).

## Sister Projects

In addition to FAIRiCUBE, the following projects have been working on
the GDDS under the HORIZON-CL6-2021-GOVERNANCE-01-17 call:

-   **AD4GD's** mission is to co-create and shape the European Green
    Deal Data Space as an open hub for FAIR data and standards-based
    services that support the key priorities of pollution, biodiversity
    and climate change. The focus will be on interoperability concepts
    that bridge the semantic and technology gaps which currently prevent
    stakeholders and application domains from multi-disciplinary and
    multi-scale access to data, and which impede the exploitation of
    processing services, and processing platforms at different levels
    including Cloud, HPC and edge computing. Grant agreement ID:
    101061001.

-   The **USAGE** project provides solutions and mechanisms for making
    city-level environmental and climate data available to everyone
    based on FAIR principles, like: innovative governance mechanisms,
    consolidated arrangements, AI-based tools, and data analytics to
    share, access, and use city-level data from Earth Observation (EO),
    Internet of Things (IoT), authoritative and crowdsources, leveraging
    on standards for data and service interoperability. The USAGE
    project has received funding from the European Union's Horizon 2020
    Framework Programme for Research and Innovation under the Grant
    Agreement no 101059950.

-   **B-Cubed** aims to make data more accessible, interoperable, and
    usable, particularly to support evidence-based policy and deliver
    public value. It builds biodiversity data cubes that can be used to
    integrate in-situ biodiversity observations with other Earth
    observation data. These cubes are the basis for harmonised,
    policy-ready indicators to track past, current and future
    biodiversity scenarios. To ensure its tools meet users' needs and
    effectively capture relevant policy aspects of biodiversity change,
    B-Cubed is conducting four case studies, which launched in
    January 2025. These cover different locations varying in geographic
    extent, biodiversity richness and data availability. Grant agreement
    ID: 101059592.

## Synergies

Looking at the descriptions of the four sibling projects, while there
are many overlaps due to the common focus on the GDDS, we also see
various synergies due to the different focus of the individual projects.
Some of these differences can be summarized as follows:

-   **Gridded vs. vector data:** while in reality all projects to a
    certain extent rely on both types of data, the focus of the
    individual projects is varied. FAIRiCUBE put its focus on gridded
    data models, where necessary transforming existing vector data to
    grids before further processing. USAGE use case focused on vector
    data, while AD4GD utilized both vector and gridded data.

-   **Provision:** related the focus on vector vs. gridded data, the
    sister projects utilized different APIs and services to expose
    available data. AD4GD, USAGE and FAIRiCUBE laid their focus on
    various OGC standards, including OGC Web Coverage Service (WCS) and
    OGC API -- Coverage for gridded data, OGC Web Feature Service (WFS)
    and OGC API -- Features for vector data, OGC SensorThings API (STA)
    for observational data, and OGC Web Map Service (WMS) or OGC API --
    Tiles for simple visualization. In contrast, B-Cubed has bypassed
    the OGC world, focusing more on the specific biodiversity standards
    from GBIF[^1] and Biodiversity Information Standards (TDWG)[^2].

-   **Thematic domain:** all sister projects had an environmental focus,
    but the specific areas under investigation were widely spread. On
    the one end of this spectrum, B-Cubed focused purely on biodiversity
    topics, whereas FAIRiCUBE more strongly integrated concepts from the
    built-up environment. Thus, between the four sister projects, a wide
    range of data types from many different domains where utilized,
    highlighting the diversity of the GDDS.

-   **Addressed stakeholder groups:** across the four sibling projects,
    a wide array of stakeholder groups was address. This ranged from
    academia over NGOs to administration, and from environmental
    scientists to technical support staff. Some projects also integrated
    citizen science aspects.

By leveraging the joint insights across all four sister projects, it was
possible for us to gain a good overview of the diverse requirements to
the GDDS.

## Activities

Based on the potential synergies between the four sister projects, it
rapidly became clear that while each project is great on its own, the
true benefit is to be gained in joining forces, merging our insights,
and supporting each other where deficits where identified. Collaboration
was started through regular monthly meetings, allowing the project
communicators to touch base and align communication activities, the
creation of joint social media postings. Over time it became clear that
more in-depth collaboration would be valuable. This led both to the
creation of the EuroGEO Action Group, as well as triggering the first of
what became a series of virtual and face-to-face workshops, both on
specific topics as well as general collaboration options.

As hoped, these joint workshops were very productive, not only
supporting all four sister projects in better performing their project
work and aligning approaches between the projects, but also in
identifying further collaboration opportunities such as the connector
collaboration work, the formulation of joint Policy Brief and its
subsequent launch at our joint final event.

Details of these individual activities are described in the following
sections.

# Meetings

## Regular Communication Meetings

Soon after the start of the projects monthly communication meetings were
set up with members of the communication and dissemination work packages
(WPs) of each project. These meetings were meant to inform each other of
ongoing and planned communication and dissemination actions and share
and highlight posts and publications. Later on, these meetings were also
used to plan and organise joint events such as workshops, the GDDS event
in Vienna (24/25.06.2025) and jointly write the policy brief.

## Occurrence Cube Workshop

FAIRiCUBE hosted a workshop dedicated to the creation and provision of
Species Occurrence Cubes at the Natural History Museum in Vienna. This
workshop took place on the 10^th^ and 11^th^ of August 2023 in Vienna,
together with representatives from the sister projects.

Occurrence Cubes are an emerging format to summarize species occurrence
data in gridded formats, as well as make it easier to reflect such
initial point/vector-based data against the vast holdings of gridded
data being generated by initiatives such as Copernicus. The goal of this
meeting was to develop an aligned approach for the creation of
Occurrence Cubes, enabling interoperability beyond the individual
projects proposing such an approach, and enabling better analysis and
processing of biodiversity data for all.

In addition to more general points on the rationale for Occurrence Cubes
and relevant governance aspects, the following topics were discussed:

-   **Required Dimensions:** in addition to the spatiotemporal
    > dimensions native to geospatial datacubes, Occurrence Cubes create
    > a requirement for a taxonomic dimension. In some cases, further
    > dimensions may be required for concepts such as the record source
    > (specimen vs. observation) or collection/collector concepts.

-   **Measures:** there are different approaches to collating the
    > information being provided within Occurrence Cubes, with some
    > paradigms only indicating presence/absence, while others utilize
    > different algorithms to generate a count. Additionally,
    > spatiotemporal uncertainty and sampling bias must be taken into
    > account.

-   **Data Formats:** at present, models for gridded data are in flux,
    > the old OGC formats have issues and are being retired while the
    > new formats are still emerging. As AD4GD and FAIRiCUBE partners
    > are involved in OGC activities, they will provide guidance.

-   **Metadata:** while each project is using a different approach at
    > metadata provision, all show deficits when providing detailed
    > information on generated data, with these concepts being relegated
    > to free-text keywords. This topic definitely deserves future work.

-   **Accessibility:** related to data formats, must is currently in
    > flux pertaining to provision of gridded data. Diverse approaches
    > are being trialled by different projects.

-   **Collaboration:** as the different projects have different
    > strengths and weaknesses, it became increasingly clear that the
    > best way forward is by helping each other.

## GDDS Requirements Workshop 

In the following year, FAIRiCUBE hosted a more general workshop on data
provision within the GDDS for the sister projects. This workshop was
again hosted at the Natural History Museum in Vienna, taking place from
September 30^th^ to October 1^st^ 2024. This event was scheduled to
coincide with the Open Earth Monitor - Global Workshop 2024 (October
2^nd^ to October 4^th^, 2024, Laxenburg, Austria)

In order to prepare the scope of this event, we first had preparatory
calls to discover the various pain points being encountered by the
sister projects. Based on these discussions, the following topics were
selected as areas of potential collaboration:

-   **Metadata:** provision of rich metadata is a core FAIR principle,
    > core to all 4 sister projects. Based on the different systems for
    > metadata provision used by these projects (GeoDCAT, ISO 19115,
    > STAC, EBV Portal), transformation modalities between these
    > metadata provision options are essential. Additionally, all
    > metadata systems have been shown to be lacking in providing
    > relevant information on the data content.

-   **Observable Properties:** the Observable Property indicates what
    > exactly the numbers provided in the data represent, e.g.
    > temperature, wind-speed or population count. To date, these are
    > often only provided as free-text keywords, making data
    > identification difficult. Emerging approaches such as the I-ADOPT
    > Observable Property Ontology investigated.

-   **Data Provision:** while European initiatives such as OpenAIRE and
    > Zenodo have made it far easier to persist project outcomes, this
    > system is only partially satisfactory when it comes to storing and
    > providing generated datasets. This is partially due to a lack of
    > well-structured metadata describing the content, partially due to
    > the lack of subsetting functionality, essential when dealing with
    > large datasets.

-   **Processing:** discussions on processing where twofold. One part
    > focused on understanding the resource requirements to a processing
    > routine before run-time, allowing for correct estimation of
    > processing requirements for planned projects. The second part of
    > this discussion was related to metadata, how to provide correct
    > provenance information on datasets, including both underlying
    > datasets and the processing routines applied. Integration of the
    > PROV-O Ontology within metadata systems would be a good support
    > for the provenance issue.

-   **Dissemination/Outreach:** in addition to the more general
    > outreach, we agreed to create a joint Policy Brief, to be launched
    > at a final event linked to the ESA Living Planet Symposium
    > (Vienna, Austria, on 23^rd^ --27^th^ June 2025)

## I-ADOPT Workshops

Based on the outcomes of the previous joint workshops and the interests
of the partners, the Observable Property topic was further persued,
whereby the focus was on the I-ADOPT[^3] Ontology for Observable
Property atomization.

I-ADOPT was developed under the RDA InteroperAble Descriptions of
Observable Property Terminology WG (RDA I-ADOPT WG), with the goal of
working on the development of a common method to systematically express
or represent Observable Properties (also refered to as Variables). This
group collected user stories, formalized into use cases, as well as
performing a survey of existing observation-centric terminologies in
order to derive use cases requirements. The semantic representation of
Observable Properties was then reflected against these requirements, and
the I-ADOPT Interoperability Framework was created.

The I-ADOPT Framework Ontology is a lightweight ontology primarily
designed to facilitate interoperability between existing variable
description models (including ontologies, taxonomy, and structured
controlled vocabularies). In 2023 the ontology was extended to support
the aggregation of variables using a range of user-defined coarser
concepts to facilitate dataset discovery and aggregation into products.

Figure 1: I-ADOPT Example shows an example of I-ADOPT variable
decomposition, based on the Observable Property "Dissolved nitrate molar
concentration per unit volune in precipitation water". In this example,
this Observable Property is decomposed into the following components:

-   **Property**: Molar concentration per unit volume

-   **Object of Interest**: Nitrate

-   **Constraint** on Object of Interest: dissolved

-   **Matrix**: Precipitation

Currently this RDA group is further validating the applicability of the
resulting I-ADOPT Ontology, creating design patterns to support in the
creation of well-structured Observable Property descriptions. To this
purpose, two workshops were organized in the spring of 2025, hosted
jointly with the OGC OMS SWG, with travel funding obtained from RDA
TIGER. Participants at these workshops included representatives from
diverse environmental domains and organizations, including participants
from the sister projects.

### WS1

In the first I-ADOPT Variable Modeling Workshop[^4] (February 20&21 at
the Museum of Natural History, Vienna) we introduced and discussed
different relevant models that can be combined to represent
observations: OMS, SensorThings API, a SensorThingsAPI generation
service, and finally also the I-ADOPT model. One critical issue, heavily
discussed, was how to connect the feature type of OMS with a specific
description component in I-ADOPT. There is no easy answer and no easy
pattern to follow although initially the ContextObject was identified to
be the most appropriate. We clarified the notion of the Matrix to be an
element rather than an object that might be measured in time and space.
We have abandoned the idea that Matrix must be included in every
variable which includes a ContextObject. A specific use case is how to
handle distance variables where we identified symmetric and asymmetric
scenarios. We discussed the potential modification of the I-ADOPT model
to allow Constraints on Properties and identified a few us cases to be
described before we can decide. Special attention was given to the
concept of "product", which could complement I-ADOPT to allow the
description of observations with information on how these variables
should be properly monitored, i.e. adding spatial and temporal
resolution triggered by the EBV case study.

These and other patterns are being described in the patterns
repository[^5]. We decided to continue working on case studies around
biodiversity, marine, and freshwater using GitHub issues[^6] and a
template[^7] that allows us to include annotations to address additional
OMS concepts. A side activity was the upload of I-ADOPT aligned EBVs in
EcoPortal (as they are also uploaded in OGC Rainbow server) and the
small project to start a variable definition pipeline using
nanopublications via EcoPortal. A key result is that I-ADOPT can be used
to give the OMS observable property FAIR context information for data
streams. However, I-ADOPT can also be used independently to enrich
metadata.

### WS2

In the second I-ADOPT Variable Modelling Workshop (March 10&11 at the
Environment Agency Austria, Vienna) we presented the outcomes and
patterns[^8] we discovered during the first workshop and introduced an
initial workflow for the development of an LLM-enabled annotation
service based on I-ADOPT[^9]. The variables in focus were provided by
the meteorological and atmospheric domain. After a presentation on the
current developments and efforts in WMO on the WIGOS Metadata
Representation[^10], we discussed specific complex variables and their
decomposition. This process led to Application Design Records
(ADRs)[^11] which list options for each encountered design problem and
on which the I-ADOPT core team will vote and agree to provide reusable
patterns. We also identified rules of alignment[^12] between the
property type in I-ADOPT and the type of the Feature of Interest in OMS
based on examples of the I-ADOPT Challenge analysed, but further
analysis is required on a bigger set of examples. A key insight of the
modelling discussion revealed the requirement to model a system of
elements for any entity in I-ADOPT and these elements can be of any
I-ADOPT nature (variables, properties, entities). In addition, we agreed
on the requirement to include statistical modifiers (maximum, average,
...) in the description. One issue was not yet addressed in detail but
will need more attention later: the further formalization of constraints
to allow better machine readability.

A common practice of domain-specific vocabularies applying I-ADOPT (see
ACTRIS vocabulary[^13] is to design I-ADOPT roles as classes which is
only possible if the same concepts appear in the same position in the
descriptions. This might be possible for a specific domain but requires
clear modelling guidance for the domain with key demo examples provided
by leading authorities like WMO. In addition, we further discussed
properties required for the product description, where ISO 10131 (Data
Product Specification - DPS) can give some directions but the real
implementation will most likely not go via ISO but via OMS SWG.

The I-ADOPT service developer team defined a harmonised roadmap[^14]
identifying tasks needed to implement the service. The service should
allow decomposing a human-readable variable description in I-ADOPT
description elements, recognize existing I-ADOPT variables and propose
them to the user or annotate the variable elements with concepts from
semantic artefacts. Two approaches will be followed:

-   using a LLM to recognise the variable concepts, and a mapping tool
    > to export them to RDF;

-   using a LLM to transform directly to RDF.

The main challenge will be how to automatically evaluate the correctness
of the results, including the reuse of adequate concepts from semantic
artefacts.

# Outreach Activities

## EuroGEO Action Group

The Action Group on the Green Deal Data Space[^15] was founded in the
[EuroGEO workshop 2022](https://www.greekgeo.noa.gr/egw2022/) (November
8th, in Athens) by the four projects working on the GDDS under the
HORIZON-CL6-2021-GOVERNANCE-01-17 call, with the objective of defining
how the GDDS could contribute to the Group on Earth Observations (GEO).
The group has a multifaceted agenda, including understanding GDDS
through current use cases, consolidating various perspectives into a
blueprint, exploring avenues for integrating the blueprint into the GEO
Work Program and GEO platform, discussing ideas regarding GDDS
governance, and outlining future development plans. The ultimate goal is
to release a set of recommendations in a concise policy document.

This action group has been extended by further projects working on
crafting the GDDS. In addition to the four projects funded by
DG-Research to develop the GDDS, this includes the following projects
financed by other DG\'s to implement data spaces:

-   **SAGE - The Data Space for a Sustainable Green Europe** (DIGITAL
    Simple Grants) (Richard Stevens, IDC): SAGE will establish a fully
    operational Green Deal Data Space (GDDS) aimed at enhancing the
    accessibility, integration, and utilization of green and
    environmental data across the EU to support key pillars of the
    European Green Deal---Zero Pollution, Climate Adaptation,
    Biodiversity, and the Circular Economy Action Plan. SAGE\'s outcomes
    include seamlessly integrating fragmented environmental data through
    federation, enriching data with consistent quality, validation, and
    interoperable metadata, and enhancing capabilities for data
    transformation, processing, analysis, forecasting, target setting,
    and performance monitoring.

-   **GREAT - The Green Deal Data Space Foundation and its Community of
    Practice** (Preparatory Action) (Richard Stevens, IDC): Funded by
    the Digital Europe program, aims to establish the Green Deal Data
    Space Foundation and its Community of Practice which builds on both
    the European Green Deal and the EU's Strategy for Data. The project
    will deliver a roadmap for implementing and deploying the Green Deal
    Data Space, an infrastructure that will allow data providers and
    initiatives to openly share their data to tackle climate change in a
    multidisciplinary manner.

-   **Data Spaces Support Center (DSSC)** (participated by IDSA
    https://internationaldataspaces.org/ and Gaia-X
    https://www.data-infrastructure.eu/): The project will set up and
    operate a Support Centre to operationalize the European Strategy for
    Data. This DSSC will facilitate common data spaces that collectively
    create an interoperable data sharing environment, to enable data
    reuse within and across sectors, fully respecting EU values, and
    contributing to the European economy and society.

In addition, the following EC research projects contributing to the GDDS
have joined the GDDS Action Group:

-   **OEMC project - Open Earth Monitor** (Tom Hengl, OpenGeoHub):
    Today's Earth observation (EO) data provides fast complex
    information concerning various changes on Earth. Could we use this
    data to protect our planet? The EU-funded OEMC project will provide
    the open-Earth-monitor cyberinfrastructure to accelerate Europe's
    capability to process high-quality, user-friendly, environmental
    information, based on Earth observation (EO) data. The developed
    cyber-infrastructure will be secured in FAIR data principles and
    existing platforms related to EO will be leveraged to a higher
    level. This will allow the monitoring of essential biodiversity
    indicators and the registering of natural capital accounts for
    private and public sectors, allowing businesses to improve their
    competitive advantage through the EU Green Deal and European
    citizens to have a better quality of life.

-   **EO4EU project** - AI-augmented ecosystem for Earth Observation
    data accessibility with Extended reality User Interfaces for Service
    and data exploitation (Stathes Hadjiefthymiades, University of
    Athens): Vast amounts of data are produced each day and become
    available via various channels, significantly enhancing our lives.
    The EU-funded EO4EU project will focus on Earth observation (EO)
    data, aiming to make it accessible to a larger audience, from
    professionals to citizens. To that end, it will deliver a set of
    advanced tools and methods leveraging AI and the FAIR data
    principles that will bridge the gap between domain experts and end
    users, encouraging wider EO data exploitation. Serverless data
    processing will further boost the EO data market, allowing
    comprehensible information modelling for a broad range of data.

-   **EIFFEL - GEOSS applications for climate change** (Dimitris
    Bliziotis, ISENSE & Yannis Kopsinis, LIBRA): Earth observation (EO)
    relies on space-based and ground-based data, which requires advanced
    processing applications. The EU-funded EIFFEL project will deliver
    innovative applications to the EO-based community that can exploit
    existing GEOSS and external datasets with minimal data collection
    operations. The project will design added-value services
    interoperable with GEOSS, using AI-based cognitive search and
    metadata augmentation tools to extract meaningful information and
    improve GEOSS metadata, and propose new methods for augmenting the
    spatiotemporal resolution of explored EO data to support diverse
    EIFFEL CC adaptation and mitigation applications. EIFFEL will cover
    a set of five different GEO societal benefit areas (SBAs) for
    various EU geographical and climatic regions at local, regional,
    national, cross-border and pan-European scales.

-   **ILIAD - Digital Twin of the Ocean**: A digital twin of the ocean
    allows experts to develop what-if scenarios, analysing the impact of
    measures to prevent and adapt to climate change. Integrating various
    data sources is key to formulating predictions of future
    developments in marine social-ecological systems. The EU-funded
    ILIAD project creates an interoperable, data-intensive, and
    cost-effective Digital Twin of the Ocean contributing to the
    implementation of the European Green Deal. It capitalises on the
    increasing wealth of data and advanced computing infrastructures by
    combining these diverse data in a semantically rich and
    data-agnostic approach allowing simultaneous communication with
    real-world systems and models. ILIAD will also create a marketplace
    to distribute apps, plug-ins, interfaces, raw data, citizen science
    data, synthesised information and value-added services.

# Connector Collaboration

All four projects funded under HORIZON-CL6-2021-GOVERNANCE-01-17 aimed
to "contribute to unleashing the potential of environmental and climate
data through dedicated European data spaces in line with the objectives
of the European Green Deal and the European strategy for data, by
further deploying digital and data technologies as key enablers...".
However, only AD4GD had a requirement for integrating DataSpace Data
Connectors within their project, while the other three sisters just
demonstrated what they considered to be state-of-the-art pertaining to
standardized conceptual models and APIs.

As AD4GD also has a strong focus on standardized conceptual models and
APIs, their desire was to integrate these established technologies with
DataSpace Data Connectors. For this purpose, the Eclipse DataSpace
Connectors (EDC), as proposed by the International Data Spaces
Association (IDSA)[^17], where investigated. While initial attempts
failed, due to recent updates in the EDC, it is now possible to wrap
existing datasets and APIs under the EDC, adding a welcome layer of
authentication and access control to existing infrastructure.

This breakthrough was communicated to the sister projects, leading to a
last-minute impromptu collaboration activity: Lets connect the sister
projects datasets and APIs via EDC to form a prototypical Green Deal
Data Space!

Deploying EDC is comprised of deploying the following components as
required:

-   Identity Provider

-   Consumer Connector

-   Provider Connector

-   Consumer Dashboard

-   Provider Dashboard

-   Federated Catalog

-   MinIO (AWS connector)

-   Consumer Backend

The Dashboard communicates with the Connector via the Management API
(creates assets, negotiations, etc.), while additionally accessing the
current catalog from the Federated Catalog.

The Federated Catalog in turn fetches the current catalog from the
Connector as shown in Figure 4.

![](media/image8.png){width="3.7881944444444446in"
height="1.4854166666666666in"}

The Identity Provider (DAPS) ensures trust using the OAuth 2.0 protocol.
It issues and checks DAT tokens. Both the Connectors and the Federated
Catalog rely on the Identity Provider as shown in Figure 5.

![](media/image9.png){width="4.472916666666666in"
height="1.7048611111111112in"}

In the Consumer Dashboard, when a user clicks on the \'Transfer\'
button, the dashboard sends a request to the Connector with Consumer
Backend address as a transfer callback. The Consumer backend, called as
a callback when the transfer achieves the state \'STARTED\', gets the
address of the provider connector as well as the credentials needed to
get the data as shown in Figure 6.

This activity is still ongoing, FAIRiCUBE is in the process of deploying

and B-Cubed currently don't have the technical resources to deploy EDC,
AD4GD and FAIRiCUBE partners will provide the deployed EDC components to
expose selected USAGE and B-Cubed resources.

# Final Event

During the GDDS Requirements Workshop in Vienna in the Fall of 2024, the
idea was born to organize a joint workshop linked to the Living Planet
Symposium organized by ESA in June 2025, to present our insights into
the GDDS and launch our joint Policy Brief. While the initial ambition
was to apply for a session during the symposium in which to highlight
our achievements, it soon became clear that the standard two-hour
session blocks would be far to limiting for our goal of providing in
depth information from four projects. Thus, an alternative venue
co-located with LPS was identified, allowing participants to easily
switch between LPS and our event, while giving us one and a half days to
fully present our insights.

The final event was organized on June 24^th^ (full day) and 25^th^ (half
day) at Vienna Research for Visual Computing (VRVIS), bringing together
researchers, policymakers, and technical experts to:

-   Demonstrate innovative tools for data interoperability and
    > governance;

-   Share lessons learnt across project implementations;

-   Launch a joint Policy Brief to inform future developments of the
    > Green Deal Data Space in Europe.

The agenda (overview in Table 1, full agenda[^18] available via the GDDS
Action Group site) featured hands-on demos, expert panels, and
high-level presentations from leaders in the Green Deal Data Space
implementation. In addition to project partners, international experts
in Data Space technologies provided welcome inputs, enabling all
participants to further evolve their understanding of both the potential
and pitfalls of current Data Space technologies.

lunch featuring posters, demonstrations and thematic discussions. This
allowed for lively exchanges between members of the four sister projects
and the diverse guests in attendance. The event is fully documented with
a summary and recordings available via the action group website to

Of special interest were the discussions on the applicability of EDC
technology in the GDDS context. EDC technology was developed in the
context of data sharing between companies, as done under GAIA-X.
However, data sharing between companies has different requirements and
constraints than the GDDS, that must support a much wider array of
stakeholders. One topic that ensued is the applicability of EDC in the
wider GDDS context, as this seems to contradict GDPR requirements.
Members of the SAGE (Sustainable Green Europe Data Space)[^19] project
actively participated in these discussions, ensuring integration of the
insights provided by the four sister projects within further
developments of the Green Deal Data Space.
