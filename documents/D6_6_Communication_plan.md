# Introduction

This communication plan outlines the strategies, activities, tools, and
channels used to disseminate and communicate the objectives, progress,
and results of the FAIRiCUBE project. The plan aims to reach and engage
target audiences to maximise the project\'s impact, visibility, and
alignment with Horizon Europe\'s objectives. Communication and
dissemination activities are a top priority in European collaborative
research projects funded under the Europe research and innovation
programme. The purpose of this deliverable is to describe the
communication and dissemination strategy of FAIRiCUBE, and to provide
greater visibility of the process. This deliverable describes the
communication objectives, target groups and key messages, and defines
the tools and channels used to communicate with the audience and to
disseminate project results. The scope includes all actions taken in and
outside of the project in terms of knowledge dissemination and public
communication regarding FAIRiCUBE and its results.

## Objectives of the communication plan

The main objective of the communication plan is to maximise the
promotion and sharing of project results. This is done by increasing the
visibility of the project outcomes and formulating relevant and targeted
messages through up-to-date communication media and engaging key target
groups.

The specific objectives of this communication plan are to:

-   Increase awareness of the FAIRiCUBE project and its objectives among
    key stakeholders.

-   Showcase the project\'s contribution to Horizon Europe\'s
    priorities, such as the European Green Deal, digital transition, and
    AI applications.

-   Promote knowledge transfer and collaboration with relevant industry,
    academic, and policy actors.

-   Encourage stakeholder engagement and promote awareness of the
    project outcomes.

# Target Audiences

The communication activities are tailored to address the needs and
interests of the following key audiences:

-   **Data Scientists and Researchers:** This group includes
    professionals in fields like environmental science, climatology,
    ecology, and biodiversity, as well as geoscientists who need
    advanced analytical tools and high-quality EO datasets. They would
    use the HUB for research projects that involve modelling climate
    impacts, studying ecological patterns, or monitoring environmental
    changes. The HUB\'s ability to offer robust models for data
    manipulation, access to spatial data cubes, and advanced
    computational power makes it an ideal resource for their needs.

-   **AI Researchers:** AI researchers utilise the HUB\'s data and
    computational resources to develop machine learning models and apply
    artificial intelligence techniques to EO data. They may focus on
    automating data classification, anomaly detection, or predictive
    analytics related to environmental changes. These users benefit from
    the HUB's cloud-based infrastructure, which allows them to process
    large datasets and train models more efficiently.

-   **Industry Professionals:** This group includes managers, engineers,
    and technicians working across various industries such as
    agriculture, energy, infrastructure, and environmental services.
    They require access to EO data to optimise operations, monitor
    environmental impacts, and support decision-making. For example,
    agricultural companies may use the HUB to monitor crop health, while
    infrastructure companies might use it for land-use assessments or
    construction planning.

-   **Service Providers**: These include companies and consultants that
    offer specialised services related to EO data, such as geospatial
    analysis, remote sensing, and environmental consultancy. They use
    the HUB to access a centralised database of EO datasets, leverage
    pre-built analytical tools, and streamline workflows for their
    clients. For these users, the HUB simplifies the complexity of data
    processing and enhances their service delivery by providing a
    reliable and efficient platform.

-   **Eco bureaus**: Eco bureaus, or environmental assessment
    organisations, often engage in activities like environmental impact
    assessments and ecological monitoring, particularly in the context
    of urban planning. They require access to detailed and reliable (EO)
    datasets to ensure compliance with regulations and to provide
    accurate assessments of the environmental impacts of development
    projects. For this segment, the HUB serves as a vital source of
    validated and pre-processed data, reducing the time and complexity
    involved in data collection.

-   **Policy Analysts:** Policy analysts work within think tanks,
    research institutions, and government agencies, focusing on
    analysing data to shape policies and regulations. For them, the HUB
    offers valuable access to processed EO data and insights that help
    to understand trends in climate, biodiversity, and environmental
    changes. They utilise these insights to create evidence-based
    recommendations and support the development of policies that address
    climate change, conservation, and sustainable development
    objectives.

# Key Messages

The following key messages are conveyed through the project\'s
communication activities for each stakeholder type:

## Key Messages for Data Scientists and Researchers

-   Empowering Advanced Research: FAIRiCUBE HUB provides access to
    high-quality Earth Observation (EO) datasets and robust models,
    facilitating cutting-edge research in climatology, biodiversity, and
    geosciences.

-   Seamless Data Manipulation: FAIRiCUBE HUB offers the tools needed
    for in-depth modelling and analysis of climate impacts, ecological
    patterns, and environmental changes.

-   Accelerating Scientific Discoveries: With FAIRiCUBE HUB\`s advanced
    computational capabilities and spatial data cubes, researchers can
    push the boundaries of their environmental studies and achieve
    faster, more accurate results.

## Key Messages for AI Researchers

-   Optimising AI Development: FAIRiCUBE\'s cloud-based infrastructure
    enables efficient processing of large EO datasets, providing the
    computational power required to develop and train sophisticated
    machine learning models.

-   Bridging AI and Environmental Insights: The HUB\'s resources support
    AI researchers in automating data classification, detecting
    anomalies, and conducting predictive analytics related to
    environmental changes.

-   Unlocking New Potential: FAIRiCUBE is a game-changer for AI research
    in environmental science, providing the data and tools needed to
    bring innovative AI applications to life.

## Key Messages for Industry Professionals

-   Enhancing Operational Efficiency: FAIRiCUBE delivers reliable EO
    data to support industry needs, from optimising agricultural
    practices to improving infrastructure planning.

-   Data-Driven Decision-Making: The HUB enables industries in the
    energy, agriculture, and environmental sector to monitor impacts,
    assess risks, and make informed decisions using up-to-date
    environmental data.

## Key Messages for Service Providers

-   Streamlining Service Delivery: FAIRiCUBE simplifies access to EO
    datasets, offering pre-built analytical tools that enhance the
    quality and speed of services like geospatial analysis and
    environmental consultancy.

-   Centralised Data Resource: The HUB acts as a one-stop shop for
    validated EO data, reducing the complexity of data processing and
    enabling service providers to focus on delivering value to their
    clients.

-   Competitive Edge in the Market: With FAIRiCUBE, service providers
    can offer enhanced solutions, leveraging advanced analytics and
    reliable data to address client needs more effectively.

## Key Messages for Eco Bureaus

-   Reliable Data for Compliance: FAIRiCUBE provides eco bureaus with
    access to comprehensive EO datasets, enabling accurate environmental
    impact assessments and compliance with regulations.

-   Time-Efficient Data Access: The HUB\'s pre-processed and validated
    data helps reduce time spent on data collection, allowing eco
    bureaus to focus on delivering quality assessments and reports.

-   Supporting Sustainable Development: With detailed insights from
    FAIRiCUBE, eco bureaus can better monitor ecological impacts and
    contribute to sustainable urban planning and development projects.

## Key Messages for Policy Analysts

-   Data-Driven Policy Insights: FAIRiCUBE\'s processed EO data helps
    policy analysts understand environmental trends and develop
    evidence-based recommendations for climate and biodiversity
    policies.

-   Supporting Strategic Analysis: The HUB provides high-quality data
    that informs analyses of climate risks, ecological changes, and
    sustainable development challenges.

-   Bridging Science and Policy: By translating complex environmental
    data into actionable insights, FAIRiCUBE supports policy analysts in
    crafting impactful policies and strategies.

## Communication channels: Website, newsletters and social media

The website, newsletters and social media are the main communication
channels for the project. These three channels are set up closely
together, linking to each other to ensure maximum traffic and outreach.

### Website 

The FAIRiCUBE website, developed as part of Task 6.1, was officially
launched in September 2022 and can be accessed at https://fairicube.eu/.
Serving as the primary information hub for the project, the website is
continuously updated to reflect the project\'s lifecycle and adapt to
the communication and dissemination strategy outlined in this
deliverable. Designed to maximise outreach and engagement, the website
disseminates key messages to target audiences, provides updates on
events, publications, and activities relevant to the FAIRiCUBE
community, and showcases the project\'s achievements. It also fosters
collaboration and active participation among consortium members while
serving as a vital delivery channel for project results and progress.
The website's evolving content ensures it remains a dynamic and
effective platform for supporting FAIRiCUBE\'s communication and
dissemination objectives.

### Social Media 

A LinkedIn page was created for FAIRiCUBE, and an official FAIRiCUBE X
page was created under the handle \@FAIRiCUBE_EU. After the first half
of the project X was dropped and only LinkedIn was used. WUR manages the
FAIRiCUBE LinkedIn and X accounts, ensuring regular updates and targeted
communication. These channels focus on engaging researchers, scientists,
consortium members, end-user groups, and other stakeholders, providing
them with timely and relevant information about the project. WUR works
in close collaboration with all FAIRiCUBE partners to maintain a
responsive, efficient, and proactive presence on these platforms. By
sharing updates, achievements, events, and key insights, the team
ensures that the social media strategy aligns with the project's
communication goals, fostering active engagement and building a vibrant
online community around FAIRiCUBE.

### Newsletter

The newsletter aims to inform the FAIRiCUBE community about the latest
achievements of the project. Four newsletters have been sent to
stakeholders to inform them of project updates, news, upcoming
activities and relevant events. It's possible to subscribe to the
newsletter via the FAIRiCUBE website. Two more newsletters are planned,
one for June 2025 and the final one for September 2025.

### Videos

A series of videos have been produced throughout the FAIRiCUBE project.
They can be found in the FAIRiCUBE website: [Video's -- FAIRiCUBE
(nilu.no)](https://fairicube.nilu.no/videos/)

### Knowledge Base (KB) 

FAIRiCUBE created a knowledge base (KB) site, to provide the appropriate
knowledge, based on the needs, experiences and understanding of the
project use cases. The design of the KB has considered different types
of users and the fact that some of them may not have specific or even
generic technical skills.

## Workshops and trainings 

### Seminars and webinars

Throughout the project, webinars and seminars were organised to present
project results and address encountered challenges. By involving the
research community, students, and other stakeholders, these events
encouraged interaction and feedback at various stages of development.
Early seminars introduced potential applications and gathered input from
end-users, while mid-term sessions provided updates and collected expert
suggestions for improvement. In the final stages, seminars presented
completed products to engage potential users and build interest in
future applications. These are briefly described below, and a more
detailed description can be found in deliverable *D64 Training and
innovation workshops*.

-   **NHM organised two seminars (2023)**, presenting the FAIRiCUBE
    consortium and the major project ideas of UC3 based on integrating
    quantitative earth observation data stored as data cubes with
    genomic data from worldwide Drosophila samples to conduct landscape
    genomics analyses. The first seminar was presented at the annual
    DrosEU Workshop in Wageningen/Netherlands (08.-09.05.2023), which
    was attended by an international group of population geneticists and
    evolutionary biologists investigating the evolution of different
    Drosophila species. The second seminar was held as part of the
    "Frontiers in molecular systematics" seminar series at the Natural
    History Museum Vienna, (30.05.2023) and targeted researchers from
    the museum and from other Viennese research institutions.

-   **NHM organised a third seminar (2024)** about UC3 at the annual
    DrosEU Workshop in Barcelona/Spain (08.-10.04.2024). In this
    Seminar, UC3 not only reported updates on the general project
    progress, but also presented a new synergy project with UC1, which
    focuses on urban adaptation of Drosophila in the city of Vienna as a
    combination of extensive sampling with the help of citizen
    scientists, high-resolution environmental data processed by UC1, and
    population genetic analyses carried out by UC3.

-   **S4E and NILU hosted a webinar on Use case 1 and Use case 4
    (March 2024) to gather feedback on use case** ideas based on
    stakeholder needs. After an introduction of the FAIRiCUBE project,
    the UCs presented their possible product options. Presentations were
    followed by an interactive session, during which feedback has been
    collected from stakeholders in European cities. Participants
    included Ville de Luxembourg, European Environment Agency,
    Wageningen Environmental Research, Epsilon Italia and Natural
    History Museum Vienna.

-   **WUR hosted a seminar on Use Case 2 (April 2024)**: introduction to
    the project, presentation of preliminary outcomes of UC2 so far.
    Presentations were followed by two interactive sessions. The first
    one aimed at collecting feedback from biodiversity experts on the
    environmental and agricultural variables used in the model, the main
    model components and the correlations explored so far. The second
    session was aimed to gather feedback on the possible applications of
    the model for farmland bird conservation (zoning, casual modelling
    and smart tooling). The webinar concluded with a discussion on
    broader ethical and feasibility issues: How accurate should the
    model be? What are the ethical implications of using AI for
    biodiversity conservation in agricultural landscape? How likely will
    such a model be adopted?

    **NHM hosted a seminar on Use case 5 (2025)**, to present the UC
    results to the scientific community: European universities involved
    in phytosociology research and the creation of EUNIS Habitats. After
    the presentation of the FAIRiCUBE Project, and our use case aims and
    results. an open discussion has been held with the audience to
    collect feedback on our method and the presented results and tools
    and promoting its applications in phytosociology and the
    classification of European habitats. In this regard, we encouraged
    field experts to include the method in their future research,
    particularly regarding the classification of European habitats
    connected with occurrences of typical taxa.

## Student supervision and guest lectures

FAIRiCUBE offered support for student research at both undergraduate and
graduate levels. These efforts aimed to inspire students to undertake
thesis projects aligned with FAIRiCUBE\'s themes, ensuring that future
researchers would benefit from the project's findings and resources. The
project team engaged students by organising lectures and providing
course challenges and assignments related to the use cases.

### Student assignments

In October 2024 WUR students enrolled in the Geoscripting course were
introduced to Use Case-related geospatial challenges that allowed them
to apply advanced computational and geospatial techniques. These
activities aimed to develop their technical skills while addressing
pressing societal and environmental issues. The training was structured
around three core challenges, each tied to a specific FAIRiCUBE use
case:

-   Understanding the Urban Heat Island Effect (use case 1)

-   Estimating Building Heights for Urban Planning (use case 3)

-   Mapping Invasive Species in European Cities (use case 5)

    In December 2024 WUR students have been involved through Academic
    Consultancy Training (ACT). ACT is the biggest course at WUR where
    students get to work on real cases from external partners, and it is
    a compulsory course in most Master's programs offered at WUR. In
    this course, a group of students with different expertise got a real
    challenge related to FAIRiCUBE and become consultants to develop a
    business model related to Use case 5. They worked for 4 weeks full
    time on this assignment and had regular meetings with EOX, WUR and
    NILU. In the end they presented their results.

Guest lectures presented project results and tools to students,

talks at universities and reached a diverse student audience. These
lectures focused on emerging challenges in areas such as machine
learning, data interoperability, and data cube technology, sparking
interest in project-related topics and creating pathways for students to
become involved.

## Project Description 

A project description has been developed and used in communication
material:

"FAIRiCUBE is a project funded by the European Union's Horizon research
and innovation programme. The core mission of FAIRiCUBE is to enable
players from beyond classic Earth Observation (EO) domains to provide,
access, process and share gridded data and algorithms in a FAIR and
TRUSTable manner. The project's goal is to leverage power of Machine
Learning (ML) operating on multi-thematic datacubes for a broader range
of governance and research institutions from diverse fields, who at
present cannot easily access and utilise these potent resources. The
core result is the creation of the FAIRiCUBE Hub, a crosscutting
platform and framework for data ingestion, provision, analyses,
processing and dissemination, to unleash the potential of environmental,
biodiversity and climate data through dedicated European data spaces."

# Open science 

Open science practices entail a transparent and collaborative approach
to scientific research, emphasizing the accessibility and sharing of
data, methods, and findings. By openly sharing research materials, data,
and methodologies, scientists promote reproducibility, enabling others
to verify and build upon their work. This approach fosters innovation,
accelerates scientific progress, and enhances the rigor and reliability
of research outcomes. Open science also promotes inclusivity by
facilitating broader participation in the scientific process, thereby
advancing scientific knowledge for the benefit of society as a whole. 

In FAIRiCUBE open science is enabled through the use of standard code
sharing, documentation and publication services which are by default
public. Our [GitHub repositories](https://github.com/FAIRiCUBE) as the
main storage point for all use case related documentation and
programming source code are public with the exception of repositories
containing user sensitive information or as requested by members of the
project. However, all use cases have their individual repository and

general data description needed for data ingestion, additional
description of data (keywords, formats, origin, bounds, URL to source,
license information, etc.) and the analysis & processing meta data
(description of \[pre\]processing, transformation, ML application, etc.
that has been applied to this data). Having this meta-data in place,
storing it as standardized STAC records and providing a smart and
findable access to it, is the requirement for enable reproducibility of
the UC work. Adding the output of other community apps deployed under
the FAIRiCUBE Hub (open-source visualization by Grafana,  MLflow to
document ML test, parametrization and performance, etc.) will further
enrich the meta-data description of the data and thereby the
interoperability and reusability.

# Upscaling

A draft plan has been made to develop the upscaling strategy for the use
of the FAIRiCUBE HUB. As upscaling relies on the identification and
engagement of stakeholders who are the potential users of FAIRiCUBE
products, during an interactive session in Girona WP6 we encouraged
partners to identify such users. A distinction has been made between
direct users, such as data engineers and scientist and indirect users,
such as policy makers, decision makers and the general public. To
prepare the upscale strategy WP6 has organised a (series of) workshop(s)
with FAIRiCUBE partners and several interaction moments with potential
users of FAIRiCUBE products. The workshop(s) with FAIRiCUBE partners is
aimed to brainstorm on possible datacube applications and finalise the
list of stakeholders (users).

# Synergise

There is monthly communication with the communication officers of the
sister projects. In August a workshop on biodiversity occurrence cubes
was organised in Vienna with the sister project B-Cubed. In October
2023, RAS participated in a hackathon organised by sister project AD4GD.

collaboration. In April 2024, a Hackathon organised by the sister
project B-cubed (Biodiversity Building Blocks for Policy) was attended
by FAIRiCUBE. In the final year, the four sister projects are working on
a joint policy paper and a joint event for June 2025.

# Conclusion

The FAIRiCUBE Communication plan described in this deliverable is
designed to maximise the visibility, impact, and outreach of the
project's objectives, progress and results. It ensures tailored and
effective communication strategies that resonate with the diversity of
targeted stakeholder groups, including AI data scientists, researchers,
industry professionals and service providers.

Leveraging a blend of tools, such as a dedicated website, social media,
newsletters, a knowledge base, and active participation in events, the
plan establishes a robust multi-channel approach to dissemination. The
integration of consistent branding, a structured social media calendar
and templates for partners ensures streamlined messaging across all
platforms.

Through this strategy, FAIRiCUBE not only facilitates knowledge exchange
and collaboration among stakeholders but also aligns with Horizon
Europe's goals of fostering innovation, promoting transparency, and
driving societal and environmental impact. As the project progresses,
this communication plan will remain pivotal in supporting its mission to
advance research and innovation in Earth Observation data and tools.
